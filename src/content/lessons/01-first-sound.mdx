The bansuri is just a bamboo tube with seven holes — one to blow across and six for your fingers. Everything else comes from your breath and your ear. This lesson gets you to one clean, steady note.

## Sitting and holding

Sit comfortably with a straight but relaxed back. Hold the flute to your **left** (the traditional way) with the blowing hole near your lips.

- **Left hand** covers holes 1–3 (nearest your mouth), using the index, middle and ring fingers.
- **Right hand** covers holes 4–6 with the same three fingers.
- Use the **pads** of your fingers (the flat part between tip and first joint), not the tips. A tiny leak is the most common reason a note won't sound.

<Callout type="tip">
  Close your eyes and press each hole lightly. You should feel a soft ring imprinted on each finger pad. If you only feel half a ring, slide your finger to centre it.
</Callout>

## The embouchure

Rest the blowing hole against your lower lip so the lip covers about a quarter of it. Keep your lips relaxed and gently smiling, and blow a thin, focused stream of air **across** the hole — like blowing across the top of a bottle, not into it.

Start with all holes open. Aim for a breathy "hoo". Then slowly roll the flute a few degrees towards and away from you until the breathy sound turns into a tone.

## Your first swara: Sa

On most bansuris, **Sa** is played with the top three holes closed and the bottom three open.

The seven shuddh (natural) swaras are **Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni**. Going up is **aaroh**, coming down is **avroh**.

## Lifting fingers, one at a time

From Sa, each new note is made by lifting the next finger up the flute:

<Fingering swara="S R G m" />

Ma is the trickiest — you half-cover the top hole. Then for Pa you close **every** hole and blow a little faster, which jumps the flute into the upper register. Dha and Ni follow the same idea as the lower ones:

<Fingering swara="P D N S'" />

## Hear it, then play it

Press play and follow along, one note per beat:

<Sargam label="Aaroh" notes="S R G m P D N S'" bpm={70} />
<Sargam label="Avroh" notes="S' N D P m G R S" bpm={70} />

| Swara | Hindi | Fingering (holes 1–6) | Breath |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sa | सा | ● ● ● ○ ○ ○ | medium |
| Re | रे | ● ● ○ ○ ○ ○ | medium |
| Ga | ग | ● ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ | medium |
| Ma | म | ◐ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ | medium |
| Pa | प | ● ● ● ● ● ● | stronger |
| Dha | ध | ● ● ● ● ● ○ | stronger |
| Ni | नि | ● ● ● ● ○ ○ | stronger |

<Callout type="tip">
  If Pa keeps coming out as the low Pa, don't blow harder — blow **faster**: make the lip opening a little smaller and the air stream narrower.
</Callout>

<Callout type="riyaz">
  Play the aaroh and avroh slowly with the tanpura on, holding each note for two beats: <code>S - R - G - …</code>
</Callout>

In Indian music every note gets its meaning from its relation to **Sa**. The tanpura plays Sa (and usually Pa) over and over so your ear always knows where home is.

## Listen first

Start the tanpura and just listen for a minute. Notice how the sound seems to bloom and shimmer after each pluck — that buzzing brightness is called **jawari**. The four strings are tuned Pa, Sa, Sa and a low Sa.

## Kharaj-style long tones

Now play your Sa over the drone. When you're in tune, the "beating" (a slow wah-wah wobble) between you and the tanpura disappears and the two sounds melt together.

Open the tuner while you do this. The trace should be a **flat green line** sitting on the red Sa line. Try to beat your "best hold" each day.

<Callout type="note" title="Why cents?">
  A semitone is divided into 100 cents. Within about ±15 cents most listeners hear a note as "in tune", so the tuner turns green inside that zone.
</Callout>

<Callout type="riyaz">
  Five minutes a day: hold Sa for a full breath, rest for the same time, repeat. Then try the same with Pa (all six holes closed, blown a little harder).
</Callout>
